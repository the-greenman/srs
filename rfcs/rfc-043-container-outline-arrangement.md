## Abstract

A document in the essay editor is a Container, and its paragraphs are records other documents may share. This RFC makes the Container's `memberInstanceIds` a flat, ordered outline: a list of entries `{instanceId, depth?}` that is both membership (read flat) and layout (order, with nesting derived from depth). Compositions are type-layer templates and no longer name records: `memberOrder` is retired, and a section only declares whether its order comes from the rendered container's arrangement or from a rule. `rootInstanceIds` is removed, navigation order is the root container's entry order, and the change ships as `dataModelRevision` 8 through the existing migration registry.

---

## Motivation

### Problem 1 — A document cannot be outlined, and the only order the specification offers is in the wrong layer

An essay is an outline: a section contains paragraphs, a subsection nests inside it, a writer folds a branch away. The only content sequence available is `DocumentSection.ordering.memberOrder` (RFC-015 [N+29]), a flat `uuid[]` of specific records inside a Composition. A Composition is a template, a package definition: the layer that holds Fields and Types. Writing the instance ids of particular records into it is the same mistake as adding specific content records to a Type. Measured today: the two spec Compositions each carry five such lists (30 ids per file, 60 in `srs/srs`), and one muSrs Composition carries six more (30 ids), so a document's order is held in three package files that must be edited whenever the content is reordered. Meanwhile 489 of the spec's 519 Part members (the 239 in four Parts that list none, and the 250 in five Parts beyond the 30 that are listed) have no declared order and are ordered implicitly by [N+12] at render time.

### Problem 2 — Nesting and order must not become meaning

Outlining paragraphs with `contains` edges or a parent id on the record makes a global claim: a paragraph shared by two essays would have one parent. `rfc-decision-8aed3412` ruled that out. Whatever carries the outline must be per document and claim nothing about the records. Containers already are per-document and claim nothing.

### Problem 3 — Navigation order is read from a different layer than document order

RFC-013 Change C derives the repository's navigation order from `precedes` edges over the root container's members, through Rule [N+12], because the member array was an unordered set. That makes a presentational fact (the order of the sections in the sidebar) depend on semantic edges, which RFC-015 specifically warns against: a `precedes` edge is a claim that a different order would be wrong. Today 20 `precedes` edges between root-container members across `srs/srs`, `muSrs` and `srs-programme` exist to order the sidebar; whether any is also a genuine semantic claim cannot be told from the data, so they are kept (ruling O).

### Problem 4 — Two membership lists, one of them redundant

`rootInstanceIds` and `memberInstanceIds` are both "direct membership" (RFC-034 [R1]). Once contains-derived membership is gone (RFC-034 [R4]) the roots list is a second list of the same kind of thing: 16 ids across the measured containers are named in both. Its four jobs each move to a declared field or are removed: typing (`anchorInstanceId`), identity (`identityInstanceId`), the link from a navigation section to its section container (`anchorInstanceId`, Change F), and traversal-derived membership (removed by RFC-034 [R4]).

## Proposed Changes

### The layers, and the two tests that sort a construct into one

Five layers, four constructs: the Container carries two aspects (membership, and sequence with layout), each declared and independently checkable. This table is the heart of the RFC; every change below follows from it. **Owner ruling A: this layer distinction stays the heart of the RFC.**

| Layer | Construct | What it declares | Names specific records? | May differ between two documents? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Semantic order | `precedes` relation | A claim about the records: a different order would be wrong (spec sections, process steps). Not removed; tools may browse the chain. | Yes (an edge), and global | No. One edge, true everywhere. |
| Membership | Container, `memberInstanceIds` read flat (ids only) | Which records are in this scope. | Yes (instance ids) | Yes. |
| Sequence and layout | Container, `memberInstanceIds` order and `depth` | In what order and nesting the members sit **in this document**. | Yes (instance ids) | Yes. One record may sit at different positions in different containers. |
| Composition | `Composition` / `DocumentSection` (type-layer template) | How a *kind* of document renders: sources, views, an order *source*, heading offsets. | **No** | n/a. A template is content-agnostic. |
| Projection | Rendered output | One reading of the above. | n/a | n/a |

1. **Anything that names a specific record cannot live in a package.** Packages define kinds. A Composition that lists record ids is a template with its content baked in. This is why `memberOrder` goes.
2. **Anything that must differ between two documents cannot be a global relation.** A global edge is one claim everywhere; a paragraph second in one essay and fifth in another cannot be an edge on the paragraph. This is why the arrangement is not a `precedes` or `contains` edge.

The arrangement names records (test 1: not in a Composition) and differs per document (test 2: not an edge), so it lives in the one construct that is per-document and may name records: the Container.

### Change A — `memberInstanceIds` becomes a flat ordered outline

`Container.memberInstanceIds` changes from an array of instance UUIDs to an array of **entries**. An entry has two properties and no others:

| Property | Type | Required | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `instanceId` | UUID | yes | The member this entry places. |
| `depth` | integer, minimum 0 | no | Nesting level, presentation-only. Absent means 0. Writers SHOULD omit 0. |

The list is flat. **Nesting is derived, never stored**: an entry's parent is the nearest preceding entry with a smaller depth, and an entry has no parent when no such entry exists. Two validity conditions, enforced by the validator (a schema cannot express them): the first entry has depth 0, and an entry's depth is at most one more than the previous entry's. So depth can rise one level at a time and fall any number of levels. The sequence of a container is simply the array order, which is also the pre-order of the derived forest; a flat list of ids is the case where every depth is 0. An entry together with the contiguous entries after it that are deeper than it is its **run** (the entry and its descendants).

**Membership is the entries' ids, read flat.** `direct(C)` (RFC-034 [R1]) is the set of `instanceId` values in `memberInstanceIds`, whatever their depth. An `instanceId` MUST appear exactly once. Every membership consumer reads ids and ignores `depth`: the discovery `containerId` filter (RFC-012), slices (RFC-026), validation, `effective(C)` closure, `containers_for_instance`, copy, and the MCP add and remove tools. A record may be a member of many containers and hold an independent position in each. There is no second list to keep consistent.

**Removing an entry promotes its descendants.** To remove an entry, delete it and reduce the depth of every entry in its run by one. The result is always valid: the first promoted entry takes the removed entry's depth, which the removed entry's own validity already allowed. Every operation that drops an entry without deleting its subtree (a member removal, a slice excluding a record, a `typeFilter` excluding a type) uses this one rule. **Moving a group is moving its run**: the entry and its descendants move together and keep their relative depths (Change D).

What this makes possible: an outline of any depth over one Container's members, copyable by copying one Container (a new `containerId` over the same records, with no id conflict because a container id is never an instance id). What it forbids: any other key on an entry. Applications may keep per-entry editor state (for example visibility or fold state) as application-private metadata outside this RFC; spec renderers render every entry, and no layer crossing is declared.

**Not-yet-placed material.** The spec has no draft concept. Editors that want a draft area keep separate draft containers, typed and linked by editor convention; nothing in the spec needs to change for that.

**Why the self-referential Type problem disappears (verified).** Revision 4's recursive `children` needed a Type that references itself, and `scripts/gen-metamodel-package.mjs` has no handling for one (checked across the 75 Types of the self-hosted metamodel package: none references itself). The flat entry needs only a non-recursive Type `container-entry` with two Fields (`instance_id` string/uuid, `depth` integer with minimum 0) referenced from `member_instance_ids` as an inline list: exactly the shape the generator already emits for `Note.sections` (`ref('note-section', 'inline', 'list')`). Absent-means-zero is prose and validator behaviour, not a schema `default`, because the generator has no default-value Field (`default_value` was removed by RFC-040 Change D). `member_instance_ids` changes datatype, and Field semantics are immutable, so it is a new Field (the generator numbers Fields append-only and hard-codes version 1: a new number and UUID, with number 115 retired); the new `container-entry` Type reuses the existing `instance_id` Field and adds one new Field, `depth`; `root_instance_ids` and `member_order` Fields are retired. The schema files are edited in lockstep with the generator and proven by the closure tests (Schema changes, below). No emitter prerequisite remains.

### Change B — The Composition declares an ordering *source*, never an order

`DocumentSection.ordering.memberOrder` is **retired**: removed from `composition.json`, with Invariants I-126 and I-127 (RFC-015 [N+29] and [N+30]) replaced by [R8] to [R11]. A new optional property `ordering.source` takes one of two values:

| Value | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `"rule"` (the default) | The section orders `direct(C)` by `ordering.fieldId` with its `direction` where present, otherwise by the Rule [N+12] order. **A rule section ignores `depth`**: every record renders at depth 0 (heading level `3 + depthOffset`), so a container with a nested outline rendered by a rule section is flat (later nesting in a container is silently ignored by a rule section, which is why a document container should be rendered by an arranged section). Every section that has no `memberOrder` today is unchanged. |
| `"arranged"` | The section renders the container's entries in their own sequence and at their own depth (Change C). The Composition names no record and does not know the order. |

**An arranged section binds to the container being rendered, with no `containerId` literal (ruling E).** The container being rendered is the Container the render is applied to; where the Composition declares `rootTypeRefs`, it applies to a Container only when the Container's typing anchor (`anchorInstanceId`) has a matching Type (RFC-009 Revision 6, unchanged). For this the `container-subset` source's `containerId` becomes optional: absent means the rendered container, and it MAY be absent only when `ordering.source` is `"arranged"`. A `container-subset` section that still carries a `containerId` is read exactly as today, and an arranged section with a literal renders the arrangement of the container the literal names (the literal wins when present; the rendered container applies only when it is absent); the literal-container leak in `container-subset` is out of scope and is the follow-up filed as the-greenman/srs#851 (ruling M, Open Question 3). Authors of new arranged sections SHOULD omit `containerId`.

**How the container is supplied.** The render operation's container parameter (the CLI `--container`, and its WASM and MCP equivalents) is the binding. An arranged section without `containerId` renders that container; if none is supplied the section is an error diagnostic and renders nothing. One Composition may mix sections with and without a literal. A supplied container that differs from a section's literal is a diagnostic, and the supplied container governs: the container being rendered overrides the literal, which is today's precedence, kept by owner ruling Q (2026-10-01). Where the Composition declares `rootTypeRefs`, the anchor match above decides whether the Composition applies to the supplied container ([R9]).

`"arranged"` is valid only on a `container-subset` section, MUST NOT be combined with `ordering.fieldId`, and MUST NOT be combined with `containerScope: "subtree"` ([R10]); each is checkable from the Composition alone, so each is a package-validation error. `ordering.direction` and `typeFilter` apply to an arranged section. Other consumers (an editor, agent context, an outline export) do not go through a Composition: they read the arrangement through the core service ([R15]).

What this makes possible: one Composition renders any container's arrangement, so a template for "an essay" never needs editing when an essay is reordered, and it names no content. What it costs: the Composition can no longer pin an order for a container it renders.

### Change C — Rendering an arranged section

An implementation MUST produce an arranged section's sequence by these steps, in order:

1. **Take** the container's entries in array order.
2. **Filter.** Remove every entry whose record is excluded by the section's `typeFilter` (silently), each by the promoting removal of Change A. An entry's **effective depth** is its depth after all removals, so excluding an entry never hides what is arranged under it.
3. **Reverse** when `ordering.direction` is `"desc"`: derive the forest from the effective depths, reverse every sibling list at every level (children stay under their parent), and flatten in pre-order. Effective depths do not change.
4. **Render** each entry in sequence at its effective depth.

An entry at effective depth `d` renders its record's heading at level `3 + depthOffset + d`, with the clamp RFC-042 [R23] specifies: a computed level above 6 is emitted at 6 with a diagnostic and the render continues (this clamp applies to markdown, html and adoc; JSON carries `depth` unclamped). At depth 0 this is today's level, so a flat migrated arrangement renders byte-identically.

**Every entry renders.** The spec defines no per-entry visibility: a renderer renders every entry that `typeFilter` does not exclude, whatever an application may keep as private editor state. For `format: "json"` the projection carries the effective depth on `ProjectedRecord` as optional `depth` (present when above 0), in sequence, so a consumer can rebuild the outline from the flat list.

**Interaction with the `contains` recursion.** RFC-042 Change G renders a record's `contains` children inside the record's own content. That is unchanged and is not the arrangement; arrangement descendants render after the entry's whole content, at effective depth plus 1. Both are layout and the clamp applies to both.

**Worked example.** A Container's `memberInstanceIds`, in order, with depth: P (0), Q (1), R (2), S (1), T (0). Derived: Q is P's child, R is Q's child, S is P's child, T has no parent. The section is `container-subset`, `source: "arranged"`, `containerId` absent, no `typeFilter`, ascending, `depthOffset` 0. The sequence is P, Q, R, S, T, with markdown heading levels 3, 4, 5, 4, 3. Had `typeFilter` excluded S's type, S would vanish and nothing else would move. Had it excluded Q's type, Q would vanish and R would be promoted to effective depth 1 (heading level 4). In JSON the five records appear in that order, Q carrying `depth: 1`, R `depth: 2`, S `depth: 1`.

### Change D — Member operations (normative)

Each operation produces a new list that MUST satisfy the validity conditions of Change A and exactly-once; an operation whose result would not is rejected whole, with an error diagnostic, and changes nothing. No operation touches a record, a relation, or any other container ([R13]).

| Operation | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **add** | With no position, **appends** the entry at the end of the list at depth 0. This is the default for every adapter. |
| **insert** | Places an entry at a given position and depth (`depth` defaults to 0). No other entry's depth changes, so entries that follow the new entry and are deeper than it become its descendants. Rejected if the result violates [R2] (the depth exceeds the previous entry's depth plus one, or the next entry would then be more than one deeper than the new one); no other adjustment is made. |
| **remove** | Deletes the entry and promotes its descendants by one (Change A). The id leaves membership. |
| **move** | Moves the entry's whole run to a position (given against the list without the run) and depth; the run's depths shift together by the same amount. Entries that follow the destination and are deeper than the moved entry become its descendants, as for insert. |
| **set depth** | Shifts the entry's whole run (indent or outdent a group) so the entry takes the given depth. A run does not include later shallower-or-equal siblings, so an entry outdented to depth `d` adopts any directly following entries deeper than `d`, as for insert. The result is validated against [R2] with no other adjustment: outdenting a depth-2 entry to 0 in front of a depth-2 entry gives 0 then 2 and is rejected. |
| **repair** | Removes every entry whose `instanceId` does not resolve to an instance, each by the promoting removal, and reports each one. Idempotent: a second run changes nothing. Its removal of an unresolved id is exempt from the pointer guard below; it never changes `identityInstanceId` or `anchorInstanceId`, so a pointer to a vanished record stays a reported validation error, because re-pointing it is a semantic choice for a human or an application. |

**Operations against pointers and the root container.** A mutating operation that removes the entry named by `identityInstanceId` or `anchorInstanceId` (remove, the record-delete cascade that removes a deleted record's entries from containers, or building a slice that excludes it) is rejected, with an error diagnostic, until the pointer is moved to another member; this applies to both pointers in every container. A render-time `typeFilter` exclusion is not a mutation and is never rejected on this ground. In the root container, an insert, move or set depth that would give the identity entry a descendant is rejected ([R12]). A promoting removal in the root container can raise a depth-1 entry to depth 0; this is the stated consequence of the navigation role being read from position, and it is why the operation reports the entries it promoted.

MCP consequence: `container_member_add` takes optional `position` and `depth` and appends at depth 0 by default; `container_member_remove` applies the promoting removal; two tools are added (`container_member_move`, covering move and set depth, and `container_member_repair`). The CLI and WASM carry the same operations from the one core service ([R15]). Operation inputs and outputs (argument names, the shape of the rejection diagnostic) are fixed by the srs-rust payload contract and not by this RFC; this RFC fixes the behaviour and the rejection rule.

### Change E — Navigation order is the root container's entry order; Rule [N+12] is retired as the navigation rule (ruling F)

RFC-013 Change C derives navigation order by projecting the Rule [N+12] order onto the non-identity members of the root container, because the members were an unordered set. Quoting its [R5]: *"A consumer MUST derive navigation order by projecting the existing Rule [N+12] order ... onto the non-identity members of the root container ... and MUST NOT derive order from the position of ids within `rootInstanceIds`/`memberInstanceIds` (which is an unordered set ...)."* This RFC replaces that rule.

**Navigation is baked into the root container's entry order.** The navigation outline is the entries of `manifest.container.memberInstanceIds` in array order, excluding the entry named by `identityInstanceId`; depth-0 entries are the sections and deeper entries are nested under their derived parent. This is a declared, root-container-only exception to "position carries no role" ([R5]): in the root container, and only there, the entry order is the navigation order and a depth-0 entry has the navigation-section role. The identity entry MUST be at depth 0 and MUST have no descendants in the root container, so excluding it never hides or reparents anything. The root container otherwise needs no special case; it moves from `rootInstanceIds` plus `memberInstanceIds` to the one list.

**What navigation shows of nested entries is an application choice (ruling J).** The spec defines the outline; a client MAY present it as a nested tree with depth honoured, or as the top-level list only. Neither is normative, and this RFC does not make nested display a conformance requirement. What the core must do is carry the outline: the navigation payload carries the entries in order with their `depth`, so a client that wants the tree has it and a client that wants the top level filters on `depth` 0; the sameness the rule requires is of the entry sequence ([R12]). A reason the spec stays out of it: navigation below a section already follows the part-of tree with `precedes` siblings (srs-rust `repository_navigation_service.rs`, `rfc-decision-0750c62f` consequence 3), so a client that nests outline entries is choosing between two sources of children for one node.

**What this retires and what it keeps.** Deleted: RFC-013 Change C and [R5] as the navigation rule, and the ordering-ownership table row that names `precedes` as navigation order, and the `createdAt`/`instanceId` tiebreak machinery Revisions 3 and 4 of RFC-013 added only because `precedes` is a partial order. Retained unchanged: Rule [N+12] itself (RFC-001 Revision 9, RFC-008) as the order of a **rule** section; `precedes` as semantic order; navigation below a section following the part-of tree with `precedes` siblings (RFC-042). The 20 `precedes` edges between root-container members are not deleted by this RFC (ruling O).

### Change F — Consumers of membership, and canonical form

Every consumer reads ids and ignores `depth` ([R3]). The cases that needed a statement:

- **Discovery** (RFC-012 [R6]): the `containerId` filter's three conditions collapse to one: the instance's id appears among the entries of the container, or, under RFC-034 [R8]'s scoping vocabulary (unchanged), of any container in `effective(C)`. Membership of each container in the closure is read from its entry ids; the closure itself is not changed by this RFC.
- **Slices** (RFC-026 Change C steps 1, 2, 6; RFC-034 [R9]): the closure tests read ids from entries. A slice keeps, in every container it contains (the root container and each descendant container in the closure), the entries for the records the slice includes, using the promoting removal for each excluded record, so every container in the slice is itself a valid outline.
- **The section-container link** (a fourth job of `rootInstanceIds`): srs-rust's navigation builds `rootInstanceId -> containerId` from `rootInstanceIds` to give each section and container member a `sectionContainerId` (srs-rust#949), and the container view reads its root from `rootInstanceIds[0]` and lists roots first. After removal the declared field is `anchorInstanceId`: **the section container of a record is the Container whose `anchorInstanceId` equals the record's id**; the container's root is its anchor entry, and members are in entry order only (the roots-first prefix goes: a root is no longer a separate list, so it sits wherever the arrangement puts it). Where more than one container names the same anchor the link is ambiguous: an implementation MUST report a diagnostic and MUST NOT choose by position or storage order. Measured: no anchor is shared, and every `muSrs` root is the anchor of the container that is its section container, so no section-container link is lost; 8 `muSrs` roots sit in several containers (the root container and their own), where today's link is a last-wins map (nondeterministic) and the anchor rule picks the container the record anchors; one `srs/srs` container has no anchor and receives one in migration step 2; the one multi-root `muSrs` container is the root container and is already anchored ([R19]). RFC-013 [R6] (a section root SHOULD be the root of some Container) becomes "SHOULD be the anchor of some Container"; RFC-034's structural coherence (a rooted child's roots SHOULD be in `direct(parent)`) becomes "a child's anchor SHOULD be in `direct(parent)`".
- **Archive and canonical form** (RFC-038 [R14], archive determinism): a canonicalizer, archive writer or enumeration MUST NOT sort or otherwise reorder `memberInstanceIds` entries; order is data ([R6]).
- **Copy of a document is copy of its container**: a new `containerId`, the same entries. Records are shared, not copied; the order and depth travel; the copy's arrangement is independent afterwards.
- **Three-way merge** (RFC-010 Revision 5 merged members as sets): the member sets still merge as sets. An arrangement counts as **changed** by a side when the relative order or depth of the ids common to the base and that side differs from the base. If neither side changed it, the base arrangement is kept; if one did, the merged set takes that side's arrangement; ids added by either side are appended at depth 0 after the arranged entries, ordered by the side that added them (ours, then theirs) and then by `instanceId`, and ids removed by a side are removed by the promoting removal. Where **both** sides changed the arrangement differently the whole container is one `container-arrangement` conflict, resolved by choosing one side's arrangement of the merged set ([R14]). A finer entry-level merge is deferred until two-origin editing of one document is real. Cost stated: two writers reordering one container concurrently conflict.

### Change G — Stale entries: a reversal of I-126 step 2, with a repair operation

Under [N+29] a `memberOrder` id that named an instance no longer in the container was silently skipped with a diagnostic, not a failure (RFC-015's Diagnostic asymmetry section: stale entries are *"expected and recoverable"*). That leniency is reversed. Because the entry list is the membership, an entry naming an `instanceId` that does not resolve is a validation **error** on the Container (RFC-013 [R2] for the root container, extended to every container by [R2]). The spec does not leave that as a manual clean-up: the **repair** operation of Change D removes every such entry with its descendants promoted and reports each, so automated cleanup is defined and conforming ([R20]). The migration (Change I) reports every `memberOrder` id it drops because it is not a member (measured: 0 of 90). One strictness is new and is stated: RFC-013 [R2] and I-80 required ids to resolve for the root container only, and [R2] here requires it of every container; a corpus with dangling ids in a non-root container is therefore newly invalid, and repair is the remedy (measured: none in the six corpora).

### Change H — Why ordered position does not contradict srs#446 and RFC-013 [R5]

srs#446 replaced a positional *typing* meaning with an explicit field: *"replace `rootInstanceIds[0]` positional significance with an **explicit anchor field** ..., making the arrays genuinely unordered as RFC-013 [R5] states."* The defect it removed was a **role inferred from an undeclared position**: nothing in the schema said index 0 meant "typing anchor". That is location over declaration.

This RFC does not reintroduce that. `memberInstanceIds` is declared to carry order: its definition says its array position is the layout, so position is the declaration. Position still carries no *role*, with the single declared exception of [R5]: the typing anchor stays `anchorInstanceId`, the identity stays `identityInstanceId`, and the root container's depth-0 entries are the navigation sections because [R12] says so, not because a reader had to know. The text "unordered set" in RFC-013 [R5] and RFC-034 [R3] was true of bare ids and is false of an ordered list, so both are amended: membership is still a set (an id appears once); order is a declared property of `memberInstanceIds`; and no order may be read from `childContainerIds` (RFC-034 [R3] alone states that). With `rootInstanceIds` removed, the migration writes `anchorInstanceId` explicitly where the I-145 fallback applied, which is the migration srs#446 itself prescribed.

### Change I — Compatibility, migration and the revision bump (ruling G)

The shape of `memberInstanceIds` changes, `rootInstanceIds` goes, and `memberOrder` goes, so data valid under revision 7 is invalid under the new schemas. This is a breaking change in the sense of RFC-033 and takes `dataModelRevision` from **7 to 8**. The migration is **an entry in the existing srs-rust migration registry** (ADR-032, `migration_registry_service.rs`), exposed through the CLI and WASM like the others, with the normal revision choreography (`srs/CLAUDE.md`: supported-revision constant, registry entry, fixture test, then release, spec PR, one pin advance). It is retired afterwards under the retirement policy of the-greenman/srs-rust#1138 (a migration is deleted once every known repository is past its revision; after that the binary refuses older corpora with a diagnostic naming the last build that can migrate them). The Revision 4 design (a one-off script, and a binary that refuses revision 7 with no registry entry) is dropped.

**Contract.** The entry's status is `needed` while the manifest's `dataModelRevision` is below 8 or any container still carries `rootInstanceIds` or string members or any Composition still carries `memberOrder`; `alreadyApplied` otherwise. Like `graduated-at-cleanup` and `revisions-sidecar-cleanup` it MUST work on the raw tree and not through the checked catalog, because an unmigrated manifest or container fails the revision-8 schemas on load. A revision-8 binary does not interpret revision-7 container shapes: for such a corpus it reports the migration as `needed` and refuses other operations with a diagnostic naming it. Because the registry is reachable from WASM, `srs-web` runs the status check when it opens a user's exploded tree and applies the migration in the browser (writing back through its normal save path); if the apply refuses, the refusal diagnostic is shown, never an empty render. This closes the "client renders empty silently on a corpus cutover" failure.

**Ladder placement, atomicity and the starting point.** Every entry in the ladder states what it requires first; this one is ordered after `discovery-query-cutover` and requires that Compositions already be in their revision-7 shape (and, through it, the whole earlier chain). Revision 7 is the minimum starting point for a revision-8 binary: a corpus below revision 7 migrates to 7 with the last build before this change, which the refusal diagnostic names (all six measured repositories are stamped 7). The apply is all-or-nothing (ADR-021 rollback), and every refusal below is decided before the first write, because the apply touches containers, packages, installed copies and the manifest stamp. "The binary loads old AND new revisions" in the revision-bump choreography (`srs/CLAUDE.md`, step 1) is met by this registry's raw-tree reader, as it was for revisions 6 and 7, and not by deserialising old shapes elsewhere; that sentence of step 1 gets a clarifying amendment in the same change.

**Archives and bundles.** A `.srsj` archive, a `.srspkg` and a `package-bundle` are pre-load bundle-format forms, which ADR-032's scope rule keeps out of the registry (the `migrate_rfc014` precedent). They are migrated by a pre-load transformer in its own module, applying the same outcome rules ([R17]); the registry entry handles the file tree and the JsonStore tree.

**What the apply does**, per repository ([R17] states only the outcome):

1. For each Container (`manifest.container` and every container file), form the member set as `rootInstanceIds` plus `memberInstanceIds`, deduplicated, and order it by the order in effect today: ids a Composition section's `memberOrder` names for this container first, in listed order, then the remaining members in Rule [N+12] order (by the same ordering function the renderer and navigation call today, with `instanceId` as the final tiebreak only where that function leaves a tie). For the root container the order is today's navigation order, which sorts the non-identity members only: the identity entry is written first and the non-identity members follow in that order, so the sidebar does not move; a `memberOrder` list that names the root container and disagrees with it is refused and reported. For any other container the leftover members are ordered over the container's full member set, as the migration cannot know a section's `typeFilter`; where a section's `typeFilter` would make today's render order differ from the frozen one (today's code sorts the leftovers over the filtered set), the gate below fails and the apply refuses. None of these occurs in the measured corpora (no `memberOrder` section carries a `typeFilter`, and no `precedes` edge touches an identity). Write flat entries at depth 0. Where one container is named by two sections with different `memberOrder` lists, refuse and report. None of these refusals occurs in the measured corpora.
2. Where `anchorInstanceId` is absent and `rootInstanceIds` is non-empty, set it to `rootInstanceIds[0]` (the fallback I-145 already applies today, so no typing check starts firing that did not fire before). Measured: 1 container in the six corpora (in `srs/srs`) and 11 in the `srs-rust` fixture. A container with several roots takes the first as its anchor and is reported by id, because it newly becomes anchored for RFC-042 [R22] nesting, which applied only to a single root; none needs this in the measured corpora (the one muSrs container with several roots is the root container and is already anchored). Remove `rootInstanceIds`.
3. For every Composition in the tree (a repository package or an installed copy; bundle forms are the transformer's): on each `container-subset` section with a `memberOrder`, remove it and set `ordering.source` to `"arranged"`. The section keeps any `containerId` it has. A `memberOrder` section combined with `containerScope: "subtree"` is refused and reported, because the result would be invalid under [R10]; a `memberOrder` on a section that is not `container-subset` (ignored with a diagnostic under [N+29]) is stripped and reported, and the section is not flipped to `arranged`. None occurs in the measured corpora. An installed package whose upstream is tracked (RFC-014) is patched locally and its drift from upstream is reported; the upstream package must publish a migrated version (the muSrs `the-spine-reader` from the `argument` package is the one case).
4. Report each `memberOrder` id dropped because it is not a member (Change G).
5. Stamp `dataModelRevision` 8 on the manifest and on every package manifest and bundle.
6. Gate (a fixture test and the corpus gate): render every Composition before and after and require byte-identical output; compare navigation order before and after. The gate also compares each record's section-container link (`sectionContainerId`) and each container view's members before and after, and tolerates and reports exactly two differences: the roots-first member prefix of the container view, and a link that was ambiguous before (a root in several containers, or an anchor that differs from `rootInstanceIds[0]`); any other difference aborts the apply with no writes. The gate needs the revision-7 ordering and render behaviour, so the registry module keeps a private raw-tree reader for the revision-7 shapes, deleted with the entry under srs-rust#1138; [R16] binds the binary's general code paths, not this module.

**Authoring step for the spec's own Compositions (ruling A, a spec change in the same PR, not part of the registry entry).** The registry entry converts only sections that had a `memberOrder` (16). The spec's two Compositions also hold 8 container-subset sections with no `memberOrder` (the four Parts "Reading this specification", "Distribution", "Presentation" and "Governance", in each Composition). Under ruling A there is no carve-out, so those 8 are flipped to `arranged` too; since the migration already froze their [N+12] order into the containers, the render is byte-identical. The five spec scripts that read order or membership change with it, each stated so that none silently loses a check: `check-spec-rendered-order.mjs` computes the order from the contains/precedes tree and must instead read the container entry order that now renders; `check-spec-coherence.mjs` reads the root container's ids as a set (now the entry ids) and keeps its `precedes`-based part-order check, which stays a semantic claim; `part-container-membership.mjs` compares declared membership as a sorted set and compares entry ids as a set, ignoring order; `render-invariants.mjs` orders Parts by [N+12] and must read the root container's entry order; `check-publication-reachability.mjs` reads `identityInstanceId`, `memberInstanceIds` and `rootInstanceIds` as publication roots and reads entry ids only.

**Landing order per repository.** `srs` and `muDemocracy.org` pin an exact binary (`SRS_RUST_CLI_TAG`, `ensure-srs-cli.mjs`) and `srs-web` pins bindings. Choreography steps 3 (corpus migration) and 4 (pin advance) therefore collapse per repository: one PR advances the pin and commits the migrated corpus, with the registry's output as the change. `srs-web` and `muDemocracy.org` move their pins before any corpus they read is migrated by another tool, because a pinned older build cannot read revision 8. A repository outside the measured set migrates itself by the same registry entry the first time it is opened with a revision-8 binary.

**Landing of the spec-side change (revision-bump choreography, Revision 10).** The choreography orders the steps: srs-rust support first, then a release, then the spec-side PR, then one pin advance. import fs from 'node:fs'; import path from 'node:path';
const walk = d => !fs.existsSync(d) ? [] : fs.readdirSync(d, {withFileTypes:true}).flatMap(e =>
  e.name==='node_modules'||e.name==='.git' ? [] : e.isDirectory() ? walk(path.join(d,e.name)) : e.name.endsWith('.json') ? [path.join(d,e.name)] : []);
const rd = f => { try { return JSON.parse(fs.readFileSync(f,'utf8')); } catch { return null; } };
for (const root of process.argv.slice(2)) {
  const man = rd(path.join(root,'manifest.json')); if (!man) continue;
  const cs = [man.container, ...walk(path.join(root,'containers')).map(rd)].filter(Boolean);
  const mem = c => c.memberInstanceIds ?? [], rt = c => c.rootInstanceIds ?? [];
  const withRoots = cs.filter(c => rt(c).length);
  const rm = new Set([...rt(man.container), ...mem(man.container)]);
  const rel = walk(path.join(root,'relations')).map(rd).filter(Boolean).flatMap(r => Array.isArray(r) ? r : r.relations ?? [r]);
  let files = 0, sections = 0, ids = 0;
  for (const f of walk(root)) { const t = fs.readFileSync(f,'utf8'); if (!t.includes('"memberOrder"')) continue; let n = 0, e = 0;
    (function v(x){ if (x && typeof x === 'object') { if (Array.isArray(x.memberOrder)) { n++; e += x.memberOrder.length; } Object.values(x).forEach(v); } })(JSON.parse(t));
    if (n) { files++; sections += n; ids += e; } }
  console.log(JSON.stringify({ root, containers: cs.length,
    changeShape: cs.filter(c => c.memberInstanceIds || c.rootInstanceIds).length,
    withRootInstanceIds: withRoots.length,
    rootIdsAlsoMembers: cs.reduce((n,c) => n + rt(c).filter(i => mem(c).includes(i)).length, 0),
    entriesAfter: cs.reduce((n,c) => n + new Set([...rt(c), ...mem(c)]).size, 0),
    anchorMissingWithRoots: withRoots.filter(c => !c.anchorInstanceId).length,
    rootPrecedes: rel.filter(r => r.relationType === 'precedes' && rm.has(r.sourceInstanceId) && rm.has(r.targetInstanceId)).length,
    memberOrderFiles: files, sections, ids }));
}

## Conformance Rules

> **[R1]** `Container.memberInstanceIds`, when present, MUST be an array of entries. An entry MUST carry `instanceId` and MAY carry `depth` (integer, at least 0; absent means 0). An entry MUST NOT carry any other property. *(Prior text, `container.json`: "`memberInstanceIds`: array of UUID ... Additional explicit members of this scope. Part of the Container's direct membership (RFC-034 [R1]).")*
>
> **[R2]** The first entry MUST have depth 0 and every later entry's depth MUST be at most one more than the previous entry's. Each `instanceId` MUST appear exactly once and MUST resolve to an instance. In the root container the entry named by `identityInstanceId` MUST be at depth 0 and MUST have no descendants. A violation is a validation error on the Container (severity error, one diagnostic per entry; codes `arrangement-depth`, `arrangement-duplicate`, `arrangement-unresolved` and `arrangement-identity`). These conditions are enforced by the validator, not the schema.
>
> **[R3]** `direct(C)` MUST be the set of `instanceId` values of `memberInstanceIds`, read flat. Every membership consumer (container-scoped discovery, slice closure, validation, `effective(C)` closure, `containers_for_instance`, copy, add and remove) MUST read ids and MUST ignore `depth`. `identityInstanceId` and `anchorInstanceId`, when present, MUST equal an id in `direct(C)`. *(Amends RFC-034 [R1]: "exactly the duplicate-free union of its `rootInstanceIds` and `memberInstanceIds`", and RFC-013 [R3].)*
>
> **[R4]** `Container.rootInstanceIds` is removed. A Container carrying it is invalid at `dataModelRevision` 8. The `rootInstanceIds[0]` positional fallback in I-145 is withdrawn.
>
> **[R5]** Nesting is derived: an entry's parent is the nearest preceding entry with a smaller depth. Position, depth and order carry no meaning beyond layout: permuting the entries of a container MUST NOT change typing resolution (the anchor is `anchorInstanceId`), identity resolution (`identityInstanceId`), the validity of any record, or `repo validate` output other than [R2] diagnostics. **Declared exception:** in the root container only (`manifest.container`), the entry order is the navigation order and a depth-0 entry has the navigation-section role of [R12]. *(Amends RFC-013 [R5] and RFC-034 [R3]: "MUST NOT infer an ordering from either membership array" no longer applies to `memberInstanceIds`; it still applies to `childContainerIds` order.)*
>
> **[R6]** Order is data. A canonicalizer, archive writer, enumerator or any other tool MUST NOT sort or reorder `memberInstanceIds` entries. *(Amends RFC-038 [R14].)*
>
> **[R7]** Removing an entry MUST reduce by one the depth of every entry in its run and MUST be the only treatment of an entry dropped without its subtree (member removal, the record-delete cascade, slice exclusion, `typeFilter` exclusion, repair). Moving an entry MUST move its whole run, preserving relative depths. An operation whose result violates [R2], or that mutates a container so as to remove the entry named by `identityInstanceId` or `anchorInstanceId` (remove, the record-delete cascade, slice construction; repair is exempt, [R20]), MUST be rejected whole with an error diagnostic (`arrangement-pointer`) and change nothing. A render-time `typeFilter` exclusion is a virtual removal and the pointer rule does not apply to it.
>
> **[R8]** `DocumentSection.ordering.memberOrder` is retired and MUST NOT occur in a Composition at `dataModelRevision` 8. `ordering.source`, when present, MUST be `"arranged"` or `"rule"`; absent means `"rule"`. A `"rule"` `container-subset` section MUST order `direct(C)` by `ordering.fieldId` with its `direction` where present, otherwise by the Rule [N+12] order, and MUST render every record at depth 0, ignoring `depth`. *(Replaces Invariants I-126 and I-127.)*
>
> **[R9]** An `"arranged"` section MUST render the entries of its container by the steps of Change C: exclude by `typeFilter` using the promoting removal, reverse every sibling list when `direction` is `"desc"`, render in sequence. An entry at effective depth `d` MUST render its record's heading at level `3 + depthOffset + d`; a computed level above 6 MUST be emitted at 6 with a diagnostic and the render MUST NOT fail (RFC-042 [R23]). The section's container is the container supplied to the render when there is one, which overrides any literal in the section's `containerId` (ruling Q, 2026-10-01: today's precedence is kept); otherwise the container its `containerId` names; with neither the section MUST produce an error diagnostic, and a supplied container that differs from a literal MUST produce a diagnostic with the supplied container governing. *(Amends RFC-042 [R20]: "the direct members of the container it names — `rootInstanceIds` ∪ `memberInstanceIds`, RFC-034's `direct(C)`" becomes "the ids of `memberInstanceIds`", and the precedence "`ordering.memberOrder` ... otherwise `ordering.fieldId` ... otherwise Rule [N+12]" becomes "the container's arrangement where `ordering.source` is `"arranged"`, otherwise `ordering.fieldId` with its `direction`, otherwise Rule [N+12]", and RFC-042 [R22]'s single-`rootInstanceId` fallback is removed.)*
>
> **[R10]** `ordering.source: "arranged"` MUST be declared only on a `container-subset` section, MUST NOT be combined with `ordering.fieldId`, and MUST NOT be combined with `containerScope: "subtree"`. A `container-subset` section MAY omit `containerId` only when `ordering.source` is `"arranged"`; an arranged section MAY carry a `containerId` (the existing literal, ruling E's lenient form, ruling P). A validator MUST report an error for each violation from the Composition alone; the schema can only make `containerId` optional on every `container-subset` section, so the rule that it may be omitted only when arranged is enforced by the validator.
>
> **[R11]** An `"arranged"` or `"rule"` section MUST render every entry that its `typeFilter` does not exclude. The specification defines no per-entry visibility or fold state; an application MAY keep such state as application-private metadata, which MUST NOT change what a conforming renderer outputs. For `format: "json"` an implementation MUST carry the effective `depth` (when above 0) on `ProjectedRecord`.
>
> **[R12]** Navigation order MUST be the order of the entries of `manifest.container.memberInstanceIds`, excluding the entry named by `identityInstanceId`; depth-0 entries are the navigation sections and an entry of greater depth is nested under its derived parent. In the root container the identity entry is at depth 0 with no descendants ([R2]). Whether a client displays the nesting or only the depth-0 entries is not specified. A consumer MUST NOT derive navigation order from `precedes` or Rule [N+12]. The core navigation payload MUST carry the entries in that order with their `depth`. Two conforming consumers given the same repository MUST produce the same entry sequence. *(Replaces RFC-013 [R5]; amends RFC-013 Change C and its ordering-ownership row "semantic sequence (reading / navigation / document order): `precedes` relation".)*
>
> **[R13]** No arrangement property (the order or `depth` of any entry) MAY alter the validity, identity, lifecycle state or relations of any record or the contents of any other container. Converting any arrangement to a flat list MUST leave `repo validate` output unchanged except that [R2] diagnostics may disappear. `precedes` remains solely a claim of semantic order.
>
> **[R14]** In a three-way merge of a Container, member sets MUST merge as sets. A side has changed the arrangement when the relative order or depth of the ids common to the base and that side differs from the base. If neither side changed it, the base arrangement MUST be kept; if one side did, the merged set MUST take that side's arrangement. Ids added by either side MUST be appended at depth 0 (any nesting the adding side gave them is not carried), ours before theirs and then by `instanceId`, an id added by both once, and ids removed by a side MUST be removed by [R7]. Where both sides changed the arrangement differently the container MUST be reported as one `container-arrangement` conflict, resolved by choosing one side's arrangement of the merged set; no entry-level merge is performed. A merge that would remove the entry named by `identityInstanceId` or `anchorInstanceId` MUST be reported as a conflict instead. *(Amends RFC-010 Revision 5: "merge `rootInstanceIds` and `memberInstanceIds` as set-union".)*
>
> **[R15]** The core service MUST expose a container's arrangement (entries with `depth`) and the operations of Change D (add, insert, remove, move, set depth, repair), and every adapter (CLI, WASM, MCP) MUST carry them unchanged: a client reading the arrangement through any adapter MUST receive the same entries. An add with no position MUST append at depth 0. The payload shape is fixed by the srs-rust payload contract, not by this RFC.
>
> **[R16]** `dataModelRevision` 8 MUST mean: container members are entries, `rootInstanceIds` and `memberOrder` are absent, and a Composition arranges by `ordering.source`. A binary supporting only revisions up to 7 MUST refuse a revision-8 corpus. A revision-8 binary MUST NOT interpret revision-7 container shapes: it MUST report the registry migration as `needed` and refuse other operations with a diagnostic naming RFC-043 and that migration.
>
> **[R17]** The migration MUST be a registry entry (ADR-032) that operates on the raw tree, ordered after `discovery-query-cutover`, with archive and bundle forms migrated by a pre-load transformer to the same outcome. Applied to a revision-7 corpus it MUST leave every Composition's rendered output and the root container's navigation order byte-identical, MUST report every `memberOrder` id it drops (diagnostic `migration-memberorder-dropped`), and MUST refuse (`migration-memberorder-conflict`), reporting each case and before any write, when one container is named by two sections with different `memberOrder` lists, when a `memberOrder` list disagrees with the root container's navigation order, when a `memberOrder` section carries `containerScope: "subtree"`, or when the before/after gate fails beyond the differences the gate tolerates; its apply MUST be all-or-nothing. Its status on a revision-8 corpus MUST be `alreadyApplied` and applying it again MUST change nothing. Its removal follows the retirement policy of the-greenman/srs-rust#1138.
>
> **[R18]** *(Amends RFC-012 [R6] and RFC-026 Change C.)* A `containerId` filter MUST match an instance exactly when its id is an entry of the container or, under RFC-034 [R8]'s scoping vocabulary, of a container in its `effective(C)` closure; the closure is unchanged. A slice MUST keep, in every container it contains, the entries for the included records and MUST drop each excluded record's entry by [R7].
>
> **[R19]** The section container of a record MUST be the Container whose `anchorInstanceId` equals the record's id; the container's root MUST be its anchor entry (the payload field name is left to srs-rust). A container with no `anchorInstanceId` has no root and no section link, which is not an error; a pre-existing anchor that differs from `rootInstanceIds[0]` takes over the link at migration. Where more than one container names the same anchor, an implementation MUST report the diagnostic `section-container-ambiguous` and MUST NOT resolve the link by position or storage order. *(Replaces the `rootInstanceIds`-based link; amends RFC-013 [R6] and RFC-034's structural-coherence statement.)*
>
> **[R20]** A conforming implementation MUST provide a repair operation that removes every entry whose `instanceId` does not resolve, each by [R7], reports each removal, is exempt from the pointer guard of [R7] for unresolved ids, leaves `identityInstanceId` and `anchorInstanceId` untouched, and is idempotent.

**Conformance fixture.** A fixture under `conformance/arrangement/`, with the shape of `conformance/discovery/` (a `fixture-repo/` and a `scenarios.json`), is part of acceptance. Scenarios: a three-level outline and its derived parents ([R1], [R5]); the first-entry and depth-rise rules rejected ([R2]); a duplicate and an unresolvable id rejected ([R2]); membership equal to the flat ids under nested depths ([R3]); a canonicalizer and an archive round-trip preserving entry order ([R6]); removal with promotion and a move of a run ([R7]); heading levels at depths 0 to 4 and the clamp at 6, `typeFilter` excluding an entry with descendants, `desc` over an outline ([R9]); a rule section flattening a nested container ([R8]); an arranged section combined with `fieldId` or `subtree` rejected, and `containerId` omitted on a rule section rejected ([R10]); every entry rendered and `depth` carried in json ([R11]); navigation equal to the depth-0 entry order, identity entry excluded, `precedes` edges that disagree ignored ([R12]); the merge cases ([R14]); removal of an identity or anchor entry rejected, an insert that would give the root identity entry a descendant rejected, outdent adopting following deeper entries ([R7], [R12]); ## What breaks, with counts

- **Every container changes shape**: 79 containers across 6 measured repositories (12 `srs/srs`, 56 `muSrs`, 6 `srs-programme`, 4 in two private repositories, 1 `srs-web` fixture), plus 12 in the `srs-rust` fixture and 3 in the discovery conformance fixture. 56 lose `rootInstanceIds`. 2254 entries result.
- **3 Composition files, 16 sections, 90 ids** lose `memberOrder` and gain `ordering.source: "arranged"`; the spec's 8 remaining container-subset sections are flipped by the authoring step.
- **Every consumer that reads the member arrays**: `srs-rust` services (membership resolution, `containers_for_instance`, slices, navigation, rendering, discovery, merge), the MCP tools `container_member_add` and `container_member_remove` (and two new tools), the WASM bindings, the `srs-web` and `srs-vscode` clients, and the spec scripts listed in Change I.
- **Old binaries refuse new corpora** (`dataModelRevision` 8). A new binary reports an old corpus as needing the registry migration and refuses other operations; nothing is silently read.
- **Nothing breaks in rendered output or navigation order** for the measured corpora: the migration gate requires byte-identical renders and an unchanged sidebar.
- **The section-container link moves to `anchorInstanceId`** (Change F): each section root keeps its link to the container it anchors; for the 8 `muSrs` roots that sit in several containers (the root container among them) the anchor rule replaces a nondeterministic last-wins choice; the container view loses its roots-first member prefix.
- **Nothing breaks in the records**: 0 instance records change, and the 263 + 224 + 32 `precedes` edges in the three public corpora are untouched.
- **Third-party or unmeasured corpora** migrate through the same registry entry on first open with a revision-8 binary. After the entry is retired under srs-rust#1138 an older corpus is refused with a diagnostic naming the last build that can migrate it.

## Rationale

**Order belongs to the document, not to the template.** A Composition describes how a *kind* of document renders. The order of one document's paragraphs is that document's content. The Container is already the per-document object and already lists exactly the records the order is about.

**One list, not two.** If the Container listed members and a second structure listed their positions, a removal or a move would have to update both and they could drift. A single list makes membership and layout the same object: an id cannot be placed without being a member.

**A flat outline, not a tree.** Nesting is derivable from depth, so storing a parent pointer or a recursive child list adds a second representation of the same fact and, in Revision 4, required a self-referential Type the metamodel generator does not model. A flat list is the shape the generator, the validator and every membership consumer already handle; "membership is the ids read flat" costs nothing.

**Compositions connect by rule only.** A section declares where its order comes from, never what it is, and an arranged section needs no container named in the template. This is what lets one essay template render any essay.

**Navigation is baked in, with one declared exception.** The sidebar order is the presentational order of the root container's sections. Deriving it from `precedes` made a presentational fact depend on semantic edges and needed a determinism patch because `precedes` is only a partial order. An entry list is a total order by construction. The exception is stated, not implied, so the srs#446 defect (a role inferred from an undeclared position) is not reintroduced.

**Clean cut over transition.** The repository is in formation (axis 2-8), the affected corpora are few and known, and a dual accepted form would be the lingering alias `rfc-decision-628cf6c4` rules out. The registry carries the migration for as long as a known corpus needs it and then retires it by policy.

**Remove `rootInstanceIds` rather than hold it.** It can only be a second membership list. Its other jobs are done by declared fields, and keeping it would keep the positional fallback alive.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alt A — Tree inside `DocumentSection.memberOrder` (Revision 3)

Rejected by the owner: a Composition is a type-layer template, and naming specific records in a package is a layer leak. It also required editing a versioned package definition on every drag.

### Alt B — A recursive tree of nodes in `memberInstanceIds` (Revision 4)

Replaced by owner ruling B. It stored the same fact (nesting) in a second representation and needed a self-referential Type the metamodel generator does not model.

### Alt C — `contains` / `precedes` edges per document

Declined by `rfc-decision-8aed3412`: edges are global claims about records, and a record in two documents would have two parents.

### Alt D — An arrangement Record referenced by the Container

Keeps the Container free of layout data at the price of two objects per document, so a copy needs two things and the Container's members and the Record's order can disagree. Declined (ruling B).

### Alt E — Keep `rootInstanceIds`, order only `memberInstanceIds`

Smaller migration. Declined by ruling D: two membership lists, an id could sit in both.

### Alt F — Accept bare UUID strings as shorthand for a leaf entry

Keeps revision-7 containers valid with no bump. Declined: a bare string and an object are two spellings of one thing forever, against one way over many.

### Alt G — Keep Rule [N+12] as the navigation rule and add the arrangement for documents only

Declined by ruling F: it would leave a presentational fact derived from semantic edges, and a root container with two orders that must agree.

### Alt H — A draft area on the Container (Revision 4, `draftInstanceIds`)

Removed by ruling C. Drafts are an editor mechanic; the spec needs nothing for it.

### Alt I — A one-off migration script with no registry entry (Revision 4)

Dropped by ruling G. It would have departed from the revision-bump choreography, and a script cannot reach the user trees `srs-web` opens at runtime.

## Open Questions

Rulings A to P are settled and are not repeated here. No question remains open. Items 1 and 2 below were open in Revision 9 and are now ruled; items 3 to 5 are deferrals or follow-ups named so they are not rediscovered.

1. **The 20 root-container `precedes` edges (ruled, O).** KEPT. After this RFC they serve no navigation purpose; each repository may prune its own later, as a per-repository authoring call. The migration deletes none.
2. **Literal `containerId` on migrated arranged sections (ruled, P).** The lenient reading: the 16 migrated sections (and the 8 spec sections flipped by the authoring step) keep the `containerId` they already carry, and new arranged sections omit it (Change B). The strict form is delivered by the follow-up in 3, and the lenient reading is enforced by an allowlist citing that follow-up rather than left as prose (ruling P).
3. **Follow-up: the `container-subset` `containerId` literal** (the-greenman/srs#851). Out of scope here (ruling M): `container-subset` sections in `spec-document-view`, `unified-document-view`, `the-spine-reader` and many muSrs Compositions name a container by id in a type-layer template, the same shape of problem this RFC fixes for records.
4. **Deferred: an arranged section with `containerScope: "subtree"`** ([R10], ruling N). Revisit when a document needs both nested records and nested scopes; the open point is where a child Container sits relative to its anchor entry's descendants.
5. **Deferred: finer merge.** An entry-level merge of order and depth is deferred until two-origin editing of one document (RFC-014 record layer, muDemocracy #62/#63) is real ([R14]).
